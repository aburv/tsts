Designing a calmer scorekeeping flow

Notes on reducing taps without hiding the details players care about.

22 August 2026Gurunathan Ramalingam7 min read

Scorekeeping happens in the middle of play. That means the interface has to be quick enough for a busy hand and clear enough for a distracted eye. Our first version tried to show everything at once, and players told us it felt like work.

Less choice at the right moment

We mapped the common flow from the first serve to the end of a set. Most actions repeat, while only a few moments need detail. The new design makes the repeated actions obvious and keeps advanced controls one step away.

Design test: can a player record the next point without looking away from the court?

We also gave each state a stronger rhythm: confirm the point, show the new score, and return the focus to the next action. That rhythm made the experience feel quieter without making it less capable.

A foundation for contributors

The flow is now made from smaller, understandable pieces. Contributors can improve a score state, add an accessibility detail, or test a new shortcut without needing to redraw the whole experience.
