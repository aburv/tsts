Feature update

Making match history useful between games

How the community shaped a faster way to revisit rallies, scores, and turning points.

18 September 2026Gurunathan Ramalingam6 min read

A match does not end when the final point lands. Players remember the run that changed the set, the rally they want to study again, and the moment a teammate found a new rhythm. Our first match history prototype treated all of that as a single scorecard.

This month, we rebuilt it around the questions players actually ask after a game: When did the momentum shift? Which rallies did we save? What should we try differently next time?

The small change: match history now puts the shape of a game before its raw numbers, so a player can understand the story in a few seconds.

From scorecard to story

We started by making the timeline scannable. Set breaks, score changes, and saved rallies now sit on one quiet line instead of competing across separate screens. The visual hierarchy keeps the latest match close at hand while leaving older games easy to find.

That structure also gives contributors a useful place to add features. A future rally note or player tag can join the timeline without asking us to redesign the whole screen.

What we learned in the open

  • People wanted context before detail.
  • Fast loading mattered more than an elaborate animation.
  • The best feature requests included a real match and a real frustration.

Match history is still growing, but its direction is clearer now. Thanks to everyone who shared a score, opened an issue, or simply told us where the experience felt slow.
